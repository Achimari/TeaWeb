# The Tea Party

One screen, one object: an original, abstract 3D cobalt teapot and its cup on a light gallery floor. Scroll or drag to turn the teapot. Three parts of it are doors:

- **The spout** opens a **tea timer**: presets (green 2 min, oolong 3, black 4, herbal 5) or a custom time up to an hour.
- **The pot** opens **Join the line**: its lid lifts and tips open as the panel appears. A display name only, no account, no password. You see your place and can leave.
- **The cup** opens **the first five**: the first five people in yesterday's line, with the date and their order.

Built with Next.js App Router, TypeScript, three.js, Zod and optional Supabase.

For a hosted release, follow [DEPLOY.md](DEPLOY.md).

## Run locally

```bash
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open http://localhost:3000. Without Supabase settings the page runs as a **preview**. The artwork doesn't mention it; the line panel says, where you join, "Preview: your place is saved only on this device." In preview:

- The line and the first five are stored in **this browser only**. Nobody else sees them, and they are not shared between devices.
- Your identity is a random id in this browser's storage.
- The preview has no server and no scheduled job. A finished day is archived **on your first visit after midnight in Riga**, not exactly at midnight. If you don't visit for several days, the days in between show as empty.

## The object

- `public/models/teapot.glb` (≈ 1.3 MB, meshopt-compressed) is generated by `scripts/build-teapot.mjs`, its editable source. Change shapes, glazes or proportions there and run `node scripts/build-teapot.mjs`.
- The script builds one abstract sculpture, in a single satin cobalt glaze lit by broad soft light:
  - a smooth, full, low body whose rim turns over into a finished cream interior with tea inside
  - a separate lid assembly (`Lid`: a domed lid with a skirt that drops inside the opening, a cream-glazed underside, and a cream knob), seated on the rim with a hairline gap
  - one clean tapering spout with a rounded lip
  - one slim oval handle blended into the body as a single continuous cobalt surface, with no overlapping shell at either attachment
  - a round, handleless cup with a cream interior and tea
- The script refuses to build if the handle rises into the lid's path or if the spout or handle could hit the cup as the pot turns.
- Named nodes are the interaction contract: `Spout*` → timer, `Cup*` → first five, everything else in `Teapot` (body, lid, knob, handle) → line. `HitSpout` and `HitSpoutTip` are invisible, deliberately fat hit volumes, so the thin spout is easy to click from any angle. `SpoutLip`, `KnobTop` and `CupRim` mark where the captions point.

### The lid

- The lid's shape and its single path live in `src/lib/lid.ts`, shared by the model script, the page and the tests. It lifts straight up until the skirt is clear of the opening, then tips back over its far edge and settles to lean on the rim. Closing runs the same path backwards.
- It tips over the edge toward the viewer's back left, chosen at the moment it starts to open but never over the spout or the handle (the hinge stays within 40° of the pot's sides). So the open lid is seen in profile, the interior faces the viewer, and the lid never stands above another part, at any turn of the pot.
- `tests/lid.test.ts` checks every point of the lid along the whole path stays inside the opening below the rim and clear of the handle.
- The motion is a critically damped spring: no bounce, it can be reversed at any moment from wherever it is, and it lands exactly. Switching to another part mid-way closes the lid from where it is. With reduced motion the lid is simply open.

## Interaction

- **Turn:**
  - The mouse wheel or trackpad over the page turns the teapot through a full 360°, and nothing scrolls. A short ease smooths coarse wheel notches, then motion stops exactly.
  - Dragging or swiping turns it 1:1. Let go while moving and it coasts and slows to a stop; a touch catches it.
  - With the turntable focused, Left/Right turn it in 15° steps and Home faces it forward.
  - With reduced motion, turns apply instantly and nothing coasts.
- **Framing:** each frame, the camera is placed as close as it can be while every point of the set (with the lid wherever it is) stays inside the free area between the corner text (`src/lib/framing.ts`). So the resting view is large (on a 1440×900 screen the pot is about 70% of the free height) and no turn ever crops the handle, spout, base or cup; the set eases in and out a little as it turns. The free area is measured from the corner text itself, so large text never overlaps the pot.
- **Choose a part:** click or tap the part itself (raycast against the model, so it works at every angle and while the lid moves). Or use its callout: each is a real button joined to its part by a short hairline. A callout always stays within 56px of its part (`src/lib/callouts.ts`): it takes the clearest nearby spot on screen, away from the sculpture, the corner text, an open panel and the other callouts, and keeps its side unless that side becomes blocked, so it doesn't jump as the pot turns. Where space is tight, a callout with a badge stacks into a narrower block. One whose part has turned behind the pot dims. Callouts work by keyboard and screen reader.
- **Panels:** one quiet card at a time, the same size for every part so nothing jumps; a bottom sheet on phones. When a panel opens the set glides into the space that is left (beside the card, or above the sheet on phones) instead of being covered. Focus moves into the panel, and Escape closes it and returns focus to the part's caption.
- **Fit:** at normal desktop and phone sizes the page is one screen with no scrolling. At high zoom, with large text or on very short screens the page scrolls, and the wheel then scrolls the page rather than the teapot.

### The tea timer

- The timer is a **saved deadline** in this browser's storage, so it is right after a reload, in a background tab, or after the computer sleeps.
- The spout's callout shows the time left, or **Ready**.
- Notifications are never requested on load. **Notify me when it's ready** asks for permission only when pressed. A notification can only appear while the page is open, possibly in a background tab; this site has no service worker for push.

## Connected mode (shared line)

1. Create a Supabase project.
2. **If you ran an earlier, account-based version**, review and run `supabase/migrations/2026-10-20-anonymous-line.sql` first. It drops the old `profiles` and `queue_entries` tables, because accounts cannot be mapped to anonymous browser cookies. You can also remove the old `@tea-party.invalid` users under Authentication → Users.
3. Run `supabase/schema.sql` in the SQL editor.
4. Enable the **pg_cron** extension (Database → Extensions), then run `supabase/cron.sql`. **This is the scheduled job, and it is required.** It runs `rollover_queue()` five minutes past every hour; extra runs do nothing.
5. Copy `.env.example` to `.env.local` and set:
   - `NEXT_PUBLIC_TEA_MODE=supabase`
   - `SUPABASE_URL`
   - `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY` (server-only; never give it a `NEXT_PUBLIC_` prefix)
   - optionally `NEXT_PUBLIC_BUSINESS_TIMEZONE`, which must match `app_settings.business_timezone` in the database.
6. Restart the server.

### How the anonymous line works

- **Identity:** joining issues a random 256-bit token in an **HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax cookie** (Secure in production). The database stores only its SHA-256 hash, so a database read cannot be turned back into someone's cookie.
- **Database access:** the browser never talks to Supabase. Only the API routes (`/api/queue`, `/api/archive`) call SQL functions with the service-role key. Every table has row-level security and no policies, and the functions are not executable by the `anon` or `authenticated` roles.
- **One place per guest per day:** a unique `(guest_key, business_date)` constraint enforces it. A double submit, a retry or a second tab returns the existing place. Order is first come, first served by join time, then insertion order.
- **Leaving:** uses the key from the caller's own cookie, never from the request body, so nobody can remove someone else.
- **Validation:** display names must be 2–24 characters (letters in any script, numbers, spaces and `.-'_`), with no markup or control characters. They are checked in the API and again by a database constraint.
- **Abuse limits:**
  - The line holds at most 500 people a day (`app_settings.line_capacity`).
  - The API allows 12 joins or leaves per 10 minutes and 120 reads per minute per client address.
- **Updates:** while the line panel is open it refreshes every 15 seconds. Realtime was removed, because anonymous visitors would otherwise need read access to the table.

### Daily archive and reset

- The business day is the calendar date in **Europe/Riga**, daylight-saving time included; timestamps are stored in UTC.
- `rollover_queue()` archives each finished day's first five into `daily_archive` and clears that day's line. It does both in one transaction, under an advisory lock, with a primary key on `(business_date, position)`, so concurrent or repeated runs can neither erase nor duplicate results.
- It runs from pg_cron, and also at the start of every queue read and write, so a missed cron run is caught by the next visitor.
- If fewer than five people joined, only they are archived. A day with nobody shows an honest empty state.

## Checks

```bash
npm test           # timer, rotation and coasting, lid path and spring, camera framing, callout placement, business day (DST), line and rollover, validation, guest tokens, and the SQL
npm run typecheck
npm run lint
npm run build
```

`tests/schema.test.ts` runs `supabase/schema.sql` in a real Postgres (PGlite, compiled to WebAssembly). It checks the queue functions: duplicate joins, leaving only your own place, capacity, the archive at Riga midnight, retried rollovers, an empty day, and the row-level-security lock-down. PGlite is a single connection, so "concurrent" calls there run one after another; parallel safety rests on the unique constraint and the transaction-scoped lock.

## Known limits

- Connected mode has not been run against a live Supabase project from this environment. The SQL is tested in PGlite, and the API routes are type-checked but not exercised end to end.
- Rate limits are kept in memory per server instance and keyed on `X-Forwarded-For`, so they are only meaningful behind a proxy that sets that header. New anonymous guests are free to create, so the daily capacity is the real bound against flooding.
